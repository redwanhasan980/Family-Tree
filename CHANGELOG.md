## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Phase 6 (in progress), more ways to see the family:
  - **List view:** the family as an indented outline with d'Aboville numbers (1, 1.1, 1.1.2 …, in Bangla digits in Bangla), photos, years, profession, husbands and wives, and which mother or father a child is from when a parent had more than one family. Branches open and close like the tree, and long lists stay fast (only the rows on screen are drawn). The selected row adds a relative or moves a child up or down among their brothers and sisters; on a computer, rows can also be dragged. It is fully usable with the keyboard and a screen reader (arrow keys, Home/End, Enter for details, Alt+↑/↓ to move).
  - **Everyone:** the List view can also show everyone in the tree at once, as a table on a computer and cards on a phone: find people by name in either script or by nickname, sort by name, year of birth or generation, and filter by living or deceased, men or women, generation and family branch.
- Phase 5, person details and photos:
  - **Full details:** birth and death dates that can be just a year or "about", places, profession, education, up to three phone numbers, an email address, present and permanent addresses, a description, and up to 20 details of your own (such as a blood group). Fields check themselves as you type, in Bangla or English, and accept Bangla digits; the form saves only what changed, as before.
  - **Details at a glance:** the panel shows a person's details with tappable phone numbers and email, their age or age at death, and gentle warnings about unlikely dates (died before being born, a parent younger than 12, alive at over 120). *Edit* switches to the form.
  - **"This is me":** mark yourself in a tree; a star shows on your card. The choice is yours alone, not shared with the tree.
  - **Photos:** take a photo or choose one (or drop it on a computer), crop it to a square with zoom and rotation, and watch it upload with Cancel and Retry. Photos show on the tree cards, in the details panel (tap for a large view), in pickers and lists, and as the dashboard cover. Replacing, removing and undoing work like any other change; old photos are deleted from Cloudinary only once nothing can bring them back. Photos need a connection and can be turned off per tree.
  - **Server:** signed Cloudinary uploads and photo deletion for owners and editors, with rate limits; deleting a tree, an account or a discarded guest tree deletes its photos too.
- Phase 4, the Tree view and building a family:
  - **Tree view:** a tidy family tree with husbands and wives side by side (two wives on either side), children under the right mother, dashed lines for divorce or separation, and cousin marriages shown once with a link to the other card. Pan and zoom, fit and centre, top-to-bottom or left-to-right, expand and collapse with "+N" counts (big trees open three generations deep), "show the tree from here" with a way back, and colours by branch, generation or gender. Cards wrap Bangla names correctly.
  - **Adding relatives in two taps:** the "+" on the selected person (in the bottom bar on phones) or a right-click or long-press menu offers a son or daughter, a husband or wife, a father or mother, a brother or sister, or someone already in the tree. *Add children* takes several names at once, each a son or daughter, in birth order, as one undo step; pasted lists become one row per name. Likely duplicates and unlikely dates ask first.
  - **Details panel:** names in Bangla and English, nickname, gender, living or not, and birth and death dates that save themselves as you type. The family section changes how a parent is related, a marriage's status and the children's order, removes a relationship, or moves someone with everyone below them under other parents. Deleting someone (alone or with their descendants) shows an Undo toast.
  - **Undo and redo** in the header, naming what they will change, and **Find someone** (Ctrl/⌘+K or "/"), which opens the branches on the way and centres the person.
  - **Keyboard:** arrow keys move between parents, children and the people beside them; Enter opens details; C, S and P add a child, spouse or parent; Del deletes; F fits. They work with a Bangla keyboard layout too.
  - **"Not yet in the tree":** people the tree can't reach from where it starts are listed with *Show* and *Connect*.
  - **Performance:** a benchmark (`pnpm bench:tree`) shows the 500-person tree rendering in under 0.2 s and panning at 53–60 fps at 4× CPU throttle; results in `docs/PERFORMANCE.md`.
  - Detail edits now move the tree's "last change" on the dashboard (at most once a minute).
- Phase 3, managing trees and accounts:
  - **Dashboard:** your trees, live, with the root person's initials, the number of people, the last change and your role. Create a tree with the oldest person you remember, rename it or add a description, and delete it (trees with more than ten people need their name typed).
  - **Editor frame:** each tree opens with a header (tree switcher, save status, statistics, tree settings, language and account), view tabs on desktop and a bottom bar with "More" on phones. The view and focus live in the link. Details open in a side panel on desktop and a drawer on phones. An empty tree offers "Add the first person"; until the views arrive, each view lists everyone in the tree.
  - **Tree settings** (names, card colours, photos, dates, the view a tree opens with) apply at once and can be undone. **Statistics** show people, generations, living and deceased, and the earliest birth year.
  - **Guest merge:** a guest who logs in to an existing account (email or Google) chooses, tree by tree, to move their trees into it or discard them. The choice survives a closed tab, and the guest account is removed afterwards.
  - **Deleting data:** registered users can delete their account and everything they own after confirming their email and password (or Google); guests can delete their guest data and start afresh.
  - **Server API:** merge tickets, the merge, account deletion and tree deletion, with rate limiting and tests that use real emulator sign-ins.
- Phase 2, the family data model underneath the editor:
  - **People and relationships:** types and schemas with the SRS field limits, partial dates in Bangla and English, display names in either language, and search across both scripts and nicknames.
  - **The graph engine:** families grouped by the other parent, descendant and ancestor trees that handle cousin marriages and adoption, and generations, numbering and "unplaced people". Validation stops loops (nobody can become their own ancestor) and more than one biological father or mother, and warns about unlikely dates and possible duplicates.
  - **Commands:** every change is an undoable command: add a person, several children at once, a spouse, a parent or a sibling; edit details; delete a person alone or with their descendants; link existing people; and change, reorder, move or remove relationships. Quick edits to the same person merge into one undo step.
  - **Security rules** for trees, people, relationships and sharing, tested path by path. Every command is checked against them on the Firebase emulator.
  - **Stores and the tree session:** the open tree renders from the device cache first, then keeps in sync live, with a saving/saved/offline status.
  - **Demo trees** of 10, 150, 500 and 2,000 people for local development (`pnpm seed`).
- Phase 1, authentication and guest sessions. Entering the app creates a guest that survives reloads and later visits; the landing page never creates one. Sign-up with email/password or Google keeps everything by linking to the guest. Also: log-in, password reset (which never reveals whether an account exists), email verification with a resend banner, and log-out. The header gets a guest chip that explains guest storage and an account menu. Settings cover name, language and theme, and the account's choices follow it to other devices. Google sign-in inside Facebook and other in-app browsers shows an "open in your browser" guide instead.
- Server foundation for Route Handlers: `verifyRequest()` (ID token with revocation check, App Check, guest policy), a shared error format with codes, and strict request validation. App Check with reCAPTCHA v3 in production once a site key is set. `/__/auth` is proxied to Firebase for redirect sign-in on the app's own domain.
- Security rules for user profiles (`users/{uid}`) with tests, and end-to-end tests for the guest and account flows that run against the emulators in CI.
- Deployment to the owner's Contabo VPS instead of Vercel: production Docker image, `/api/health`, Docker Compose stack with Caddy (automatic HTTPS), a one-time server setup script, a deploy script with automatic rollback, a GitHub Actions deploy workflow (enabled with the `DEPLOY_ENABLED` variable), a CI image build, and the owner guide `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`.
- Phase 0 foundation: Next.js 16 app with Bangla and English routes (`/bn`, `/en`) and automatic language detection, a placeholder landing page with language and theme switches, design tokens for light and dark mode with an automated contrast check, Inter and Hind Siliguri fonts, the shadcn/ui component kit, and the product name শিকড় always shown in Bangla script.
- Developer tooling: ESLint with layering rules, Prettier, Husky and commitlint, Vitest, Playwright smoke and accessibility tests on mobile and desktop, Firebase Auth and Firestore emulators with a security-rules test harness, an emulator-only seed script, security headers, and GitHub Actions CI.
- Named the product **শিকড়**, always written in Bangla script in both languages (I18N-10); technical identifiers use the Latin `shikor` prefix.
- Planned the Family card (v1.0), Generation album (v1.1), Timeline (v1.2) and Family book (v1.2) in the SRS, Design Document and Implementation Plan. Later phases are renumbered: P11–P15.
- Project documentation baseline: Software Requirements Specification (`docs/SRS.md`), Design Document (`docs/DESIGN.md`), Implementation Plan (`docs/IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`), `CLAUDE.md`, the `family-tree-dev` Claude skill, and this README and changelog.
